# Title
Personal context: location, interests, and what is intentionally excluded

# Category
experience

# Problem
Professional profiles can become noisy if they mix high-signal career evidence with personal or unverifiable details. A curated knowledge base should include only information that is relevant and supported, and omit anything sensitive or not needed.

# My role
I am based in Winterthur, Switzerland, as stated in my CV materials. My interests listed include mastering sushi, ferroequinology, Japanese model trains, butoh, judo, football, hiking, and photography.

# What I built
I keep personal details limited to what is explicitly included in my source documents (e.g., location, languages, and a short interests list). The knowledge base is intentionally not a full biography; it focuses on evidence-backed professional experience, projects, research, and credentials.

# Scale and impact
The impact for an “Ask About Piotr” demo is better trust: the assistant can answer professional questions confidently and refuse when a question goes beyond available evidence. This mirrors good AI product behavior in enterprise settings, where controlled refusal is safer than guessing.

# Tech stack
Languages noted: Polish (mother tongue), English (fluent), German (intermediate). Location: Winterthur, Switzerland. Personal status details may exist in some CV variants but should be treated as optional for demo scope.

# Key decisions and trade-offs
The trade-off is completeness vs privacy: omitting sensitive or irrelevant details reduces risk and keeps answers grounded. When evidence is missing, the system should respond with low confidence and a controlled refusal rather than hallucinating.
